State-of-the-Art Audio AI: Case Study Resources

Five models every student of generative audio should study. This page collects the key papers, code, demos, and explainers for each model covered in our case study lectures. The models span audio compression, speech synthesis, speech recognition, and music generation—chosen because each has a published paper, open code, and represents a distinct technical paradigm.

Topic 1 — Foundation

EnCodec

Meta's neural audio codec compresses audio into discrete tokens using a convolutional encoder-decoder with residual vector quantization (RVQ). It is the shared representation layer that VALL-E, F5-TTS, and MusicGen all build on top of. Understanding EnCodec is prerequisite to understanding the generation models.

EnCodec

Meta AI · 2022 · Convolutional encoder-decoder + RVQ

Compresses 24kHz mono or 48kHz stereo audio at bitrates from 1.5 to 24 kbps, outperforming traditional codecs (Opus, EVS) at equivalent bitrates. The discrete token sequences it produces became the de facto input representation for language-model-based audio generation.

Topic 2 — TTS (Autoregressive)

VALL-E

Microsoft's landmark model reframed text-to-speech as language modeling over EnCodec tokens. A 3-second voice sample is enough for zero-shot cloning. The first RVQ level is generated autoregressively (capturing content and identity), while remaining levels are generated non-autoregressively (acoustic detail). This paradigm influenced nearly every subsequent codec-based TTS system.

VALL-E

Microsoft Research · Jan 2023 · Autoregressive codec LM

Trained on 60K hours of English speech (LibriLight). Demonstrated that treating TTS as “next-token prediction on audio codes” could achieve natural prosody and speaker similarity from a 3-second prompt—a capability that previously required minutes of reference audio and fine-tuning.

📄 Paper (arXiv) 🔊 Demo & Samples (MS Research) ⚙ UniLM GitHub (official) ⚙ VALL-E-X (community impl.) 📖 Visual Explainer (L. Bouchard) 📖 Training Tutorial (Paperspace)

VALL-E 2

Microsoft Research · Jun 2024 · Repetition-aware sampling + grouped codec modeling

Follow-up that claimed the first human-parity result on zero-shot TTS benchmarks. Introduced repetition-aware sampling to fix the looping artifacts of the original, and grouped code modeling for efficiency. Worth reading alongside the original to see how the paradigm matured.

Topic 3 — TTS (Non-Autoregressive)

F5-TTS

A fully non-autoregressive approach that uses flow matching with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to generate speech in parallel. Trained with a simple “fill in the middle” objective—masking a portion of the mel spectrogram and learning to reconstruct it—which naturally supports zero-shot voice cloning and speech editing. Contrasts sharply with VALL-E's autoregressive token-by-token generation.

F5-TTS

SWivid · Oct 2024 (ACL 2025) · Flow matching + DiT

“A Fairytaler that Fakes Fluent and Faithful Speech with Flow Matching.” Achieves competitive quality with a simpler training pipeline than autoregressive alternatives. The non-autoregressive generation means the entire utterance is produced in a fixed number of denoising steps rather than token-by-token, offering a different speed/quality tradeoff.

Topic 4 — Speech Recognition

Whisper

OpenAI's encoder-decoder transformer for automatic speech recognition (ASR). Unlike the generation models above, Whisper operates on mel spectrograms (not codec tokens) and maps audio → text. Its key lesson: a standard architecture trained on 680K hours of weakly supervised web audio can match or beat specialized ASR systems across languages without any fine-tuning.

Whisper

OpenAI · Sep 2022 · Encoder-decoder transformer on mel spectrograms

Multitask model that handles transcription, translation, language identification, and timestamp prediction in a single architecture. Available in sizes from tiny (39M params) to large-v3 (1.5B params). The “just scale up the data” result was a turning point for the ASR field and a case study in how weak supervision at scale competes with careful data curation.

Topic 5 — Music Generation

MusicGen & Suno

MusicGen is Meta's open-source, single-stage transformer for text-to-music generation, operating over EnCodec tokens with a clever delay pattern that handles multiple codebook streams in one forward pass. Suno is the leading commercial system, producing full songs with vocals, but has no published technical paper.

MusicGen

Meta AI · Jun 2023 · Single-stage autoregressive transformer over EnCodec

“Simple and Controllable Music Generation.” Generates high-quality instrumental music conditioned on text descriptions or melody input. The delay pattern—offsetting each codebook stream by one step—lets a single transformer model multiple parallel token streams without the multi-stage cascade that earlier systems (like MusicLM) required. Text conditioning uses T5 embeddings.

📄 Paper (arXiv) ⚙ AudioCraft GitHub 🤖 HuggingFace (large) 🤖 HuggingFace (melody) 🔊 AudioCraft (Meta AI) ▶ MusicGen Explained (YouTube) 📖 HuggingFace Docs
About Suno: As of 2026, Suno (currently on v6) produces the highest-quality full songs with vocals from text prompts. However, it is entirely closed-source with no published paper or architecture details. It is best understood as a demonstration of what’s commercially possible rather than a technical case study. The ongoing copyright lawsuits from major record labels make it an important discussion topic for the ethics and policy side of the course.

How the models connect: EnCodec provides the discrete audio token representation that VALL-E, F5-TTS, and MusicGen all generate over. Whisper is the exception—it operates on mel spectrograms and maps in the opposite direction (audio → text). Together, the five models illustrate autoregressive vs. non-autoregressive generation, language modeling vs. flow matching, and the role of scale in both supervised and self-supervised settings.
